1. Termination conditions, forfeiture and immaterial breach of an insurance covenant

Proposition to test. Under Georgia law a lessor who terminates under a clause conditioned on a default continuing "thirty days after written notice thereof" must strictly satisfy those conditions; lease forfeitures are disfavoured and construed against the lessor; and a variance from an insurance covenant that causes the lessor no loss (here: lessor made loss payee by endorsement back-dated to inception; lessee never named as co-payee) does not support forfeiture. Why decisive. This is Athens Count I. If Georgia enforces termination clauses as written without a materiality inquiry, the Lessee's absence as loss payee is enough for Stevens and every defense but cure-in-fact and waiver falls away. Time frame. Lease dated 2/25/1969; notice 2/9/2026; termination 3/19/2026. Searches.

  • NL: Georgia lease termination forfeiture not favored tenant failure to maintain insurance naming landlord loss payee cure after notice
  • T&C: (lease /s forfeit! /s "not favored" disfavor! strict!) & (insur! /p (covenant "loss payee" "loss payable")) & (terminat! /p notice /p cure)
  • T&C: dispossess! & lease & (insurance /s (covenant requir!)) & (terminat! /s default) & (revers! vacat!)

Authorities cited in the work under review.

  • "Mendel v. Pinkard, 108 Ga. App. 128 (1963)" — cited for: same lease as Pinkard; forfeiture not enforced; strict construction.
  • "Peachtree on Peachtree Investors, Ltd. v. Reed Drug Co., 251 Ga. 692 (1983)" — cited in our Fulton Compl. ¶ 35 for no forfeiture for "insubstantial or technical noncompliance"; the audit says its excerpts show strict construction only.
  • "O.C.G.A. § 13-4-20" — cited in the draft amended answers (Seventh Defense) for substantial performance and performance by a substituted person.
  • "O.C.G.A. § 23-1-23" — cited in the draft amended answers (Eighth Defense) for "Equity relieves against forfeitures."
  • "Tela v. Razavi (2019); Oastler (1946); Cannon (2002); Nunn v. Taylor (1985); Vinings Jubilee (2004)" — cited for waiver by acceptance; covenant vs. condition; strict compliance with § 44-7-54. Full citations are not given in the work.

What would change my answer. A controlling case enforcing termination for an uncured insurance-covenant default regardless of prejudice moves Athens from "contestable" to "depends entirely on request 2." A reversal refusing forfeiture for a cured or harmless insurance variance makes immateriality a primary defense.

3. Waiver, mutual departure, and notice before insisting on strict terms

Proposition to test. Where the parties depart from a contract term over a course of years (lessor accepts certificate-level proof, non-lessee named insureds, and insures part of the premises itself), the lessor must give reasonable notice of intent to rely on the exact terms before declaring a default; whether a departure occurred is ordinarily for the jury; and a default notice that itself demands the non-conforming performance (a "certificate") is not such notice. Why decisive. This is the answer to your "50 years" question on the corrected facts. If the 2/9 letter itself counts as the required notice and 30 days is reasonable, waiver adds nothing to cure. Time frame. Course of dealing 1993–2025; notice 2/9/2026. Searches.

  • T&C: "13-4-4" & lease & (depart! /s terms) & (notice /s (strict exact)) & (jury "question of fact")
  • T&C: (landlord lessor) /p waiv! /p (insurance /s (covenant certificate)) /p (accept! /s rent)
