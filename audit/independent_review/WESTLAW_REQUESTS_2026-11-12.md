## 1. Termination conditions, forfeiture and immaterial breach of an insurance covenant
**Proposition to test.** Under Georgia law a lessor who terminates under a clause conditioned on a default continuing "thirty days after written notice thereof" must strictly satisfy those conditions; lease forfeitures are disfavoured and construed against the lessor; and a variance from an insurance covenant that causes the lessor no loss (here: lessor made loss payee by endorsement back-dated to inception; lessee never named as co-payee) does not support forfeiture.
**Why decisive.** This is Athens Count I. If Georgia enforces termination clauses as written without a materiality inquiry, the Lessee's absence as loss payee is enough for Stevens and every defense but cure-in-fact and waiver falls away.
**Time frame.** Lease dated 2/25/1969; notice 2/9/2026; termination 3/19/2026.
**Searches.**
- NL: Georgia lease termination forfeiture not favored tenant failure to maintain insurance naming landlord loss payee cure after notice
- T&C: (lease /s forfeit! /s "not favored" disfavor! strict!) & (insur! /p (covenant "loss payee" "loss payable")) & (terminat! /p notice /p cure)
- T&C: dispossess! & lease & (insurance /s (covenant requir!)) & (terminat! /s default) & (revers! vacat!)
**Authorities cited in the work under review.**
- "Mendel v. Pinkard, 108 Ga. App. 128 (1963)" — cited for: same lease as Pinkard; forfeiture not enforced; strict construction.
- "Peachtree on Peachtree Investors, Ltd. v. Reed Drug Co., 251 Ga. 692 (1983)" — cited in our Fulton Compl. ¶ 35 for no forfeiture for "insubstantial or technical noncompliance"; the audit says its excerpts show strict construction only.
- "O.C.G.A. § 13-4-20" — cited in the draft amended answers (Seventh Defense) for substantial performance and performance by a substituted person.
- "O.C.G.A. § 23-1-23" — cited in the draft amended answers (Eighth Defense) for "Equity relieves against forfeitures."
- "Tela v. Razavi (2019); Oastler (1946); Cannon (2002); Nunn v. Taylor (1985); Vinings Jubilee (2004)" — cited for waiver by acceptance; covenant vs. condition; strict compliance with § 44-7-54. Full citations are not given in the work.
**What would change my answer.** A controlling case enforcing termination for an uncured insurance-covenant default regardless of prejudice moves Athens from "contestable" to "depends entirely on request 2." A reversal refusing forfeiture for a cured or harmless insurance variance makes immateriality a primary defense.

## 2. Agent binding, back-dated endorsements, and the certificate statute
**Proposition to test.** (a) Where an insurer's agent with binding authority adds a loss payee and requests the endorsement, the insurer is bound from that time even though the endorsement issues later; (b) an endorsement issued 4/21/2026 "effective 11/21/2025" gives the loss payee enforceable rights for the back period; (c) the statute Stevens cites makes a non-conforming certificate void but does not bar proof that coverage was in fact bound.

## 3. Waiver, mutual departure, and notice before insisting on strict terms
**Proposition to test.** Where the parties depart from a contract term over a course of years (lessor accepts certificate-level proof, non-lessee named insureds, and insures part of the premises itself), the lessor must give reasonable notice of intent to rely on the exact terms before declaring a default; whether a departure occurred is ordinarily for the jury; and a default notice that itself demands the non-conforming performance (a "certificate") is not such notice.
**Why decisive.** This is the answer to your "50 years" question on the corrected facts. If the 2/9 letter itself counts as the required notice and 30 days is reasonable, waiver adds nothing to cure.
**Time frame.** Course of dealing 1993–2025; notice 2/9/2026.
**Searches.**
- NL: Georgia mutual departure from contract terms landlord must give reasonable notice before insisting on strict compliance lease covenant
- T&C: "13-4-4" & lease & (depart! /s terms) & (notice /s (strict exact)) & (jury "question of fact")
- T&C: (landlord lessor) /p waiv! /p (insurance /s (covenant certificate)) /p (accept! /s rent)
**Authorities cited in the work under review.** "O.C.G.A. § 13-4-4" — cited for a mutual-departure notice requirement. The waiver cases listed under request 1.
**What would change my answer.** A holding that a contractual notice-and-cure letter satisfies the reasonable-notice requirement would confine waiver to the named-insured and control grounds.

## 4. Notice scope and the State Court's power over forfeiture
**Proposition to test.** (a) A lessor may not justify termination on defaults not stated in the written default notice; (b) a State Court hearing a dispossessory may apply relief from forfeiture as a defense, though it cannot grant affirmative equitable relief.
**Why decisive.** (a) removes first-named-insured control, insurable interest and "joint reconstruction management" as grounds. (b) decides whether the Eighth Defense is usable in Athens, and whether our Athens answers and our Fulton Objection ¶ 5 (State Court "cannot … relieve against forfeiture") can stand together.
