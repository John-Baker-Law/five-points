A. Executive findings — the ten most legally significant established departures

These are ranked by consequence, without regard to which side they help. Each one names the actor, the governing text, the classification, the support label, and the act-ledger IDs (§D).

  1. The policy itself never made loss "payable jointly to Lessor and Lessee" during the cure window, or ever as to the Lessee. The Prime Lease requires the Lessee to "keep the improvements insured with loss payable jointly to Lessor and Lessee" (Lease, Disp. Aff. Ex. 1; Fulton Compl. Ex. A). Harford/1842 policy BP114482810 was issued 11/21/2025 to Nu Age Horizon, LLC as sole named insured, with no loss-payee clause (BS-0129, 0134–0135). The only loss-payee endorsement (BP1203) issued 4/21/2026, after the 3/19 termination letter, with a stated effective date of 11/21/2025. It names Stevens only. Under Clause A, loss on 1655 Building 1 is payable jointly to Nu Age and Stevens; Clause D covers 1655 Building 2 and 1675 (Fulton Compl. Ex. C; MC 6160–6320). Bottle Stop, the Lessee, is never a loss payee on any policy then insuring the buildings. It appears as a loss payee (Clause C4) only on the Hanover schedules issued with the 1/20/2026 deletion of building coverage. FPC's Main Street policy named Stevens loss payee on 1655 from 11/18/2025 until its 2/28/2026 expiration, partly inside the cure window, but it was not the Lessee's policy and did not name the Lessee (B). Our own verified complaint ¶ 22 dates the loss-payee endorsement to April 21, 2026. Before 4/21 the only documents saying otherwise were certificates (ACORD EPIs of 11/21, 11/24/2025 and 2/19/2026) and the agent's letters. Classification (Bottle Stop, as Lessee obligated by the covenant): CONTRARY-TO-LAW (contractual) as to the literal joint-loss-payee term, A. Whether agent-bound coverage plus a retroactive endorsement counts as performance or cure depends on Georgia law and on binding-authority evidence that has not been produced (E). This is Stevens' strongest ground, and the "no default" contract theory is weakest here. The 2018–2021 Citizens declarations (§K) make it stronger still. On this account, the lessor's loss-payee status was historically conferred by a BP1203 policy endorsement, not by certificate, and in 2018 the tenant side obtained one within a week. Ledger I-03, I-09, I-14, N-02; §K.
  2. Stevens tied termination to the 2/9-noticed default but justified it with rationales the notice did not state and the lease does not contain. The lease permits termination only "if Lessee defaults for thirty days after written notice thereof." The 2/9/2026 notice named two defects: (a) Nu Age as owner/insured, with no certificate naming Lessor or Lessee as insured or loss payee; and (b) no coverage for "Tract A, 145 SQFT" and "Tract B, 234 SQFT." The 3/19 letter's operative sentence ties termination to that notice: "Since more than 30 days have elapsed since confirmed delivery of my letter describing Lessee's default, and such default not having been cured … the Lease is hereby terminated." In explaining why the certificates did not cure, it added rationales the notice did not state: first-named-insured control of cancellation, changes and premiums; the Lessee's omission as loss payee on the ACORD; and "Lessor and Lessee will jointly manage reconstruction after loss." On reconstruction, the lease says only that proceeds "shall be used to replace the improvements" and that the Lessee pays any excess. Joint management is not in the text, though our own Compl. ¶ 14 refers to the lease's "reconstruction-after-loss procedure." The 3/19 letter did not restate the Tract ground; Manoll revived it on 4/23. The 4/10 letter called the default "not curable." Classification (Stevens): OUTSIDE-LIMIT of the contractual termination power to the extent termination rests on any matter first raised 3/19 or later, A. The termination stands or falls on whether ground (a), the absent joint loss payee, was fairly noticed on 2/9 and remained uncured. The notice's wording ("no certificate … identifies Lessor or Lessee as insureds or loss payees") arguably noticed it. Ledger N-01–N-07.

Bottom line.
  • The lease. The lease imposes narrow insurance duties. Stevens enforced a broader set than the lease contains and expanded its grounds after notice. It did not engage with a timely cure tender. It stands to capture the improvements ("all improvements … become the property of Lessor") and a rent spread of roughly $8,292/month on 1655 alone.
  • The Lessee's weakness. On the documents, the Lessee did not have loss made "payable jointly to Lessor and Lessee" on the policy at any time before termination, and it never named the Lessee. That literal deficiency gives Stevens a defensible termination ground. The case therefore turns less on "there was no default" than on four questions:
    1. the scope of the 2/9 notice;
    2. whether agent-bound coverage and a retroactive endorsement count as cure;
    3. materiality and equitable relief from forfeiture, and which court can grant it;
    4. waiver and estoppel from rent acceptance and course of dealing. The 2018–2021 Citizens documents (§K) support this as to named insured, control and back-dating. They undercut it as to certificates.
  • Our own side. Our record has serious, correctable problems: misstated filings, unsigned verifications, unserved corrections and lapsed self-set deadlines. They weaken the equitable posture.
